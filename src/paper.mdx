<PaperFigure name="intro" alt="Comparison of LoopLM post-training approaches. GRPO supplies terminal-loop reward, RLTT extends reward across loops, and OPD requires an external teacher. ### Can a Model Teach Itself by Thinking Deeper?

In a looped language model, intermediate and terminal loops share parameters but receive different amounts of computation. The terminal loop can therefore serve as a compute privileged teacher without a larger model or privileged information. LoopOPD turns this internal compute advantage into dense, token level supervision on the student's own responses.

Both policies are evaluated on the same prompt and student generated response prefixes. The teacher's compute advantage comes from additional recurrent depth. This gives looped models a source of supervision within the model itself, even when a stronger external teacher is unavailable.

<section id="methodology" aria-labelledby="methodology-title">

<h2 id="methodology-title">Methodology</h2>

<PaperFigure name="method" alt="LoopOPD distills a frozen terminal-loop teacher into an intermediate-loop student using reverse KL on student-generated rollouts. D-LoopOPD uses the current terminal-loop policy with stop-gradient, refreshing the teacher after every shared parameter update." />

LoopOPD samples responses from the intermediate loop student and distills the terminal loop teacher's next token distributions into the student on the same prefixes. In our main experiments, **loop 2 serves as the student and loop 4 as the teacher**. The teacher in LoopOPD is a frozen copy of the initial model.

D-LoopOPD instead uses the current model's terminal loop policy, with gradients stopped through the teacher distribution. Each student update changes the shared parameters and therefore the teacher used at the next step. Repeating this process turns cross loop distillation into recurrent self-improvement.

<aside className="takeaway">
  <h3>Why Shared Parameters Matter</h3>
  <p>Updating the intermediate loop student also changes the trainable model's terminal loop policy through shared parameters. The paper characterizes this interaction and gives sufficient gradient alignment and step size conditions for a single update to improve task surrogates locally at both depths.</p>
</aside>

<section id="results" aria-labelledby="results-title">

<h2 id="results-title">Results</h2>

<Results />

On Ouro-2.6B-Thinking, D-LoopOPD raises mean Avg@16 across AIME 2024, 2025, and 2026 from **55.49% (vanilla) to 62.36%**. Its AIME 2024 score of **71.67%** is comparable to vanilla Qwen3-8B's 71.25%, with fewer parameters. The gains also extend to MMLU, MBPP, MBPP+, HumanEval, and HumanEval+, despite training only on DAPO-Math-17k.

### Improvement Across Recurrent Depths

In the main setting, only loop 2 receives direct distillation supervision, yet improvements extend across all four loop depths. On AIME 2024, D-LoopOPD improves the student loop by **8.34 percentage points** and the terminal loop by **9.59 points**. The experiments also examine how the gains depend on the decoding budget and the choice of student depth.
